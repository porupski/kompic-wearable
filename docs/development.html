Mk1 iv7.1 → Mk1b iv8.0

A hardware postmortem, logged as it happens.

Bring-up, bugs, bodges, and fixes — every stage appended here as it goes.

1. The First Reflow and Bench Bring-up (Mk1 iv7.1)

Assembly began with board revision iv7.1 — a 4-layer, 1 mm PCB with pre-baked SAC305 paste and 0402 passives assembled by JLCPCB. Main ICs were hand-soldered or hot-aired on the bench.

Stage 1 proved the core power rails: the 3.19 V main buck rail, the 1.78 V sensor LDO rail, and clean USB enumeration on the ESP32-S3. Initial testing surfaced the first trap: the PCF85063A RTC failed to respond on I²C Bus 1, because excess solder on its DFN-10 exposed center ground pad bridged to adjacent signal pins.

Stage 2's localized hot-air reflow brought up the primary sensor array, and with it two early lessons in hardware debugging:

  1. The Phantom Dead Peripheral. An "alive-but-stuck" MSM261 PDM microphone turned out not to be a dead mic chip, but a cold solder joint at the ESP32 controller pad. Reflowing the MCU pin brought the audio stream to life.
  2. Bench Magnetic Interference. The LIS3MDLTR magnetometer showed a hard −4 G full-scale saturation on its X-axis. Wave-testing with a small magnet proved the die was fine — the saturation came from magnetized bench tweezers and hot-air tools.

Sources: Stage_1_Build_Report.md, Stage_2_Build_Report.md, Schematic_Audit_Stage1_iv7.1_2026-06-18.md

2. The Bench Battles: 5 Critical Bugs of iv7.1

Stage 3 brought up the daughterboard (MAX30101 PPG + TMP117) and the microSD card slot — and the iv7.1 prototype hit a wall of critical board bugs.

The daughter board is attached using eight 0.1 mm enamel wires. While this thin wire is somewhat brittle and prone to fatigue, its compact size allows it to fit easily into the limited space.

Bug #1: The Source-Follower Flashlight

The flashlight LED was driven by a BSS138W N-FET, wired on the source side — a source-follower topology. Gate drive from the MCU could never exceed the LED forward voltage, so it never lit — instead, as above, it leaked a faint constant glow.

Bench fix: traces cut and jumpered to a low-side switch. A later batch of BSS138W FETs showed die-level sub-µA leakage causing the same glow all over again, so it was swapped for a BC847C NPN transistor.

Bug #2: The Great SDMMC "Phantom" Failure

SD card init consistently timed out on send_scr and send_op_cond. Weeks went into decoupling caps, ground bodges, and CLK series resistors before the real cause surfaced: a fresh 32 GB SDHC FAT32 card passed 65/65 at 20 MHz on the original iv7.1 routing. The "unfixable bus fault" was a dying 64 GB exFAT-formatted card.

Bugs #3–5: The Rail Killer and the Fried PPG

The XC6206 1.8 V LDO's library symbol had VIN/VOUT swapped. At the same time, VDDIO for the LSM6DSV16X and BME688 was tied to the +1V8 rail while their I²C lines ran at +3.3 V — internal ESD clamp diodes back-fed +3.3 V into +1V8, spiking it to 2.3–2.8 V and killing the MAX30101 (2.0 V absolute max).

Bench fix: the LDO was unsoldered, rotated 180°, and re-landed. The +1V8 copper branch was cut over C29, sensor VDDIO jumpered to +3.3 V, and a fresh MAX30101 fitted — it responded immediately at 0x57.

Bugs #6–7: Ship-Mode Loop & USB-C Reversibility

The BQ25619's /QON pull-up (R12) was tied to +3.3 V, so ship mode collapsed the rail to 0 V and immediately re-woke the board. Lifting R12 let the charger's internal 200 kΩ VBAT pull-up handle ship-mode cleanly. Separately, iv7.1 only routed one D+/D− pair, breaking USB-C cable reversibility.

The LIS3MDLTR also picked up an internal latched axis saturation — cleared without desoldering by rotating a neodymium magnet in a decaying circular pattern near the chip.

3. Firmware Expansion and the Fatal Display Flaw

With iv7.1's hardware stabilized via bodges, focus moved to software — porting from Arduino sketches to a modular ESP-IDF framework.

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐ │ ESP32-S3 Core Firmware Architecture │ ├───────────────────┬─────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┤ │ Power Management │ Sensor Pipelines │ User Interface & Storage │ │ - Dynamic Freq │ - PPG+BCG Twin │ - LVGL 9 Tile Engine │ │ - VBUS Light-Slp │ - Mic Capture │ - SDMMC CSV/WAV Logging │ │ - Ship-Mode Gate │ - 240Hz IMU Stream │ - 4s Hold Safety Watchdog │ └───────────────────┴─────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┘

Dynamic Frequency Scaling and VBUS-aware light-sleep dropped idle draw from 106 mA to 81 mA — a +47% runtime gain (2h 44m continuous logging). A twin PPG+BCG heart-rate pipeline landed, along with raw 200 Hz PPG/BCG recording.

Stage 16: The Showstopper

The CO5300 2.06" AMOLED display and its 24-pin FPC receptacle arrived. Soldered on, panel plugged in, test sketch flashed —

The screen stayed pitch black.

Post-mortem: the receptacle footprint on iv7.1 was drawn mirrored. Panel FPC Pin 13 (GND) landed on PCB Pin 1 (QSPI_RESET). Rotating either part by 180° didn't help — the flex cable had single-sided contacts. Flying 14 fine wires for 40 MHz QSPI signals was ruled too fragile. Display bring-up on iv7.1 was a dead end. Time to spin Mk1b iv8.0.

Sources: Stage_11–13, Stage_16_AMOLED_Receptacle_Mirror.md, Stage_17_Firmware_Polish_Prep.md

4. Re-Blueprinting: the Birth of Mk1b (iv8.0)

The board wasn't the only thing back at the drawing board. The case has been going through its own run of iterations in parallel with the PCB redesign — a titanium two-piece shell with zero assembly screws and full internal retention turns out to be its own multi-week project, on its own timeline.

5. Mk1b Bring-up, Display Triumph, and the GPS Silence Mystery

The Mk1b PCBs arrived. Assembly followed a strict order: power path → ESP32 SoC → topside I²C sensors → peripherals → AMOLED → GPS.

Footprint constraints on this revision meant the skin-facing daughter board (MAX30101 + TMP117) had to be flying-wired on with 0.1 mm enamel magnet wire — the same treatment the GPS module would need further down the line.

A minor USB D+/D− trace short on the test pads was resolved, restoring USB CDC-JTAG enumeration. Then the critical test: flash the display firmware, mate the AMOLED.

The CST9217 touch controller ACKed at 0x5A, the CO5300 initialized over QSPI in 690 ms, and the screen lit up.

The BQ25619 charger and the new MAX17048 fuel gauge came up next, correctly reporting cell voltage and state-of-charge over I²C Bus 2.

The Great GPS Silence Mystery

The u-blox MAX-M10S was bodged on with 0.1 mm enamel magnet wire and a 1 cm 50 Ω coax pigtail to the external patch antenna.

Powered up: complete silence. The scope showed clean 9600-baud squarewaves out of the module's TX pin. The same module on an ESP32-C3 SuperMini decoded NMEA perfectly. On both the Mk1 and Mk1b ESP32-S3 boards, the UART peripheral registered zero bytes.

The cause, buried in max_m10s.h:

// Incorrect configuration in firmware header: #define MAX_M10S_TX_PIN 18 // ESP32 GPIO18 #define MAX_M10S_RX_PIN 17 // ESP32 GPIO17

TX and RX were completely inverted relative to the Master Pinout spec — GPIO17 is U1TXD (ESP → GPS RX), GPIO18 is U1RXD (ESP ← GPS TX). With the header swapped, uart_set_pin() configured GPIO18 as an output and drove it straight into the GPS module's own TX pin. Every previous "swap the wires" test had moved physical wires without recompiling the inverted firmware defaults — so no combination could ever have worked.

Fixing the pin definitions unblocked it instantly:

[STAT] total=947 B window=289 B/s (last 1000 ms) NMEA=28 UBX=0 err=0 idle=418 ms rx=GPIO18 tx=GPIO17 baud=9600 clk=APB [NMEA] $GNRMC,,V,,,,,,,,,,N,V*37 [NMEA] $GNVTG,,,,,,,,,N*2E [NMEA] $GNGGA,,,,,,0,00,99.99,,,,,,*56 [NMEA] $GNGSA,A,1,,,,,,,,,,,,,99.99,99.99,99.99,1*33

Clean NMEA at ~289–370 B/s, no hardware trace cuts or pin bodges needed on the GPS UART.

6. Current Status

Kompic̄ Mk1b (iv8.0) is a fully operational wearable prototype:

  • All 10 I²C devices ACK cleanly: 0x10 VEML, 0x1C LIS, 0x36 MAX17048, 0x48 TMP, 0x51 PCF, 0x57 MAX30101, 0x5A CST/DRV, 0x6A BQ, 0x6B LSM
  • 2.06" CO5300 AMOLED + CST9217 touch drive a smooth LVGL 9 UI — auto-sleep after 15s, touch/button wake gates, Space Grotesk typography
  • MAX-M10S GPS runs reliably on UART1 (TX=17, RX=18, 1PPS=46), processing UBX-NAV-PVT frames, logging UBX-MON-RF, dynamic nav-model switching, and syncing UTC to the PCF85063A RTC on satellite lock
